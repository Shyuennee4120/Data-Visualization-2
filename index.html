Conclusion

What do these patterns reveal about Australia's reliance on cars?

Australia's transport story is shaped by its reliance on cars, but that reliance looks different across cities, states and households. Cars dominate passenger travel and commuting, while other modes contribute differently across places. Age, sex and dwelling type also reveal differences in how people travel and their access to vehicles. Together, these patterns show that understanding Australia’s road travel requires looking beyond national totals to the places, people and connections behind them.

Reading the measures

  • Passenger kilometres combine the number of people travelling with the distance travelled.
  • Vehicle kilometres measure distance travelled by vehicles, including commercial vehicles in the road-use charts.
  • Passenger movements count journeys, rather than unique people.
  • Commuting figures describe Census day, 10 August 2021, and may differ from usual travel behaviour.
  • Each chart identifies its percentage denominator. Geographic differences show associations and do not establish causes.
  • BITRE notes higher uncertainty in private-vehicle estimates after 2020; bus passenger-kilometre estimates are approximate.
